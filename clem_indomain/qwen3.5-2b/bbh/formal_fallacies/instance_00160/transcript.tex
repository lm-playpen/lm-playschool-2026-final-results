\texttt{*   \$C\$: Being a classmate of Williams.} \\
\texttt{*   \$W\$: Being a workmate of Leslie.} \\
\texttt{*   \$G\$: Being a great{-}grandfather of Joseph.} \\
\texttt{*   \$T\$: Being a cousin of Tony.} \\

\texttt{**2. Translate the Premises:**} \\
\texttt{*   **Premise 1:** "Being a classmate of Williams is necessary for not being a workmate of Leslie."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: \$W \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$ (If you are a workmate, then you are a classmate).} \\
\texttt{    *   *Note: "Necessary for" means if the consequent (\$C\$) is true, the antecedent (\$W\$) must be true. Wait, let's re{-}read carefully.*} \\
\texttt{    *   "Necessary for" usually means \$A\$ is necessary for \$B\$ means \$B \textbackslash{}rightarrow A\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   Let's re{-}evaluate the phrasing: "Being a classmate of Williams (\$C\$) is necessary for not being a workmate of Leslie (\$\textbackslash{}neg W\$)."} \\
\texttt{    *   This means: If you are not a workmate (\$\textbackslash{}neg W\$), then you must be a classmate (\$C\$).} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolically: \$\textbackslash{}neg W \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   *Alternative interpretation check:* Could it mean \$C\$ is necessary for \$\textbackslash{}neg W\$? Yes, that is \$\textbackslash{}neg W \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   Let's look at the next premise to see if the logic holds up.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Premise 2:** "Being a classmate of Williams is necessary for not being a great{-}grandfather of Joseph."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: \$\textbackslash{}neg G \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   (If you are not a great{-}grandfather, you are a classmate).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Premise 3:** "Every cousin of Tony is a workmate of Leslie and a great{-}grandfather of Joseph."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: \$T \textbackslash{}rightarrow (W \textbackslash{}land G)\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Conclusion:** "Everyone who is a cousin of Tony is a classmate of Williams, too."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: \$T \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**3. Analyze the Deduction:**} \\
\texttt{We need to prove \$T \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$.} \\
\texttt{From Premise 3, we know that if \$T\$ is true, then both \$W\$ and \$G\$ are true.} \\
\texttt{So, \$T \textbackslash{}rightarrow W\$ and \$T \textbackslash{}rightarrow G\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Now look at Premise 1: \$\textbackslash{}neg W \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$.} \\
\texttt{This is equivalent to its contrapositive: \$\textbackslash{}neg C \textbackslash{}rightarrow W\$.} \\
\texttt{This tells us: If you are *not* a classmate, you *must* be a workmate.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Now look at Premise 2: \$\textbackslash{}neg G \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$.} \\
\texttt{This is equivalent to its contrapositive: \$\textbackslash{}neg C \textbackslash{}rightarrow G\$.} \\
\texttt{This tells us: If you are *not* a classmate, you *must* be a great{-}grandfather.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**4. Combine the Logic:**} \\
\texttt{Assume \$T\$ is true (Cousin of Tony).} \\
\texttt{From Premise 3, we know \$W\$ is true AND \$G\$ is true.} \\
\texttt{From Premise 1 (\$\textbackslash{}neg W \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$), since \$W\$ is true, the antecedent (\$\textbackslash{}neg W\$) is false. In logic, if the antecedent is false, the implication is vacuously true, but it doesn't tell us about \$C\$. It only says "If not classmate, then workmate." It doesn't force us to be a classmate.} \\
\texttt{From Premise 2 (\$\textbackslash{}neg G \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$), since \$G\$ is true, the antecedent (\$\textbackslash{}neg G\$) is false. Similarly, this doesn't force us to be a classmate.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Let's try the contrapositive approach again to see if we can derive a contradiction or a specific outcome.} \\
\texttt{We know \$T \textbackslash{}rightarrow W\$ and \$T \textbackslash{}rightarrow G\$.} \\
\texttt{We know \$\textbackslash{}neg W \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$ and \$\textbackslash{}neg G \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$.} \\

\texttt{Does \$T \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$ follow?} \\
\texttt{Let's test with a counter{-}example.} \\
\texttt{Suppose there is a person who is a cousin of Tony (\$T\$).} \\
\texttt{From Premise 3, this person is a workmate (\$W\$) and a great{-}grandfather (\$G\$).} \\

The implication \$\textbackslash{}neg W \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$ does not tell us anything about whether they are a classmate (\$C\$). They could be a workmate who is NOT a classmate.} \\
