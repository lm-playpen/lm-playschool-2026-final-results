\texttt{    *   This translates to: It is not the case that (Marisa is not a classmate AND Karen is not a schoolmate).} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: \$\textbackslash{}neg(\textbackslash{}neg C \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg K)\$} \\
\texttt{    *   By De Morgan's Law, this is equivalent to: \$C \textbackslash{}lor K\$ (Marisa is a classmate OR Karen is a schoolmate).} \\

\texttt{*   **Premise 2:** "Not every sister of Ruth is a schoolmate of Karen."} \\
\texttt{    *   This translates to: It is not the case that (All sisters of Ruth are schoolmates of Karen).} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: \$\textbackslash{}neg(\textbackslash{}forall x (Sister(x, Ruth) \textbackslash{}to Schoolmate(x, Karen)))\$} \\
\texttt{    *   This implies there exists at least one person who is a sister of Ruth but not a schoolmate of Karen.} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: \$\textbackslash{}exists x (Sister(x, Ruth) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg Schoolmate(x, Karen))\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Conclusion:** "Somebody is a sister of Ruth and not a classmate of Marisa."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: \$\textbackslash{}exists x (Sister(x, Ruth) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg Classmate(x, Marisa))\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**3. Analyze the Logic:**} \\
\texttt{*   From Premise 1, we know that for any person \$x\$, if \$x\$ is a sister of Ruth, then \$x\$ must be a classmate of Marisa (\$Sister(x, Ruth) \textbackslash{}to Classmate(x, Marisa)\$).} \\
\texttt{    *   *Reasoning:* If there were a sister of Ruth who was *not* a classmate of Marisa, Premise 1 would be violated because that person would be "neither a classmate of Marisa nor a schoolmate of Karen" (assuming they aren't a schoolmate, which is the negation of the disjunction). \texttt{    *   Contrapositive: If someone IS NOT a schoolmate of Karen, they MUST be a classmate of Marisa.} \\
\texttt{    *   So, \$Sister(x, Ruth) \textbackslash{}to Classmate(x, Marisa)\$.} \\

\texttt{    3.  The conclusion claims that \$x\$ is **not a classmate of Marisa**.} \\

\texttt{    *   These two statements cannot both be true. The conclusion directly contradicts the logical consequence of the first premise.} \\
