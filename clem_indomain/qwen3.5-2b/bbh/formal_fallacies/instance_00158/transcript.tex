\texttt{*   \$C(x)\$: \$x\$ is a cousin of Samantha.} \\
\texttt{*   \$A(x)\$: \$x\$ is an ancestor of Janice.} \\

\texttt{**2. Translate the Premises:**} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Premise 1:** "Every close friend of Francine is a cousin of Samantha or an ancestor of Janice or a close friend of Shanika."} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolic: \$\textbackslash{}forall x (F(x) \textbackslash{}rightarrow (C(x) \textbackslash{}lor A(x) \textbackslash{}lor S(x)))\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Premise 2:** "Every close friend of Francine is a cousin of Samantha."} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolic: \$\textbackslash{}forall x (F(x) \textbackslash{}rightarrow C(x))\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Premise 3:** "Being a close friend of Shanika is necessary for being a close friend of Francine."} \\
\texttt{    *   "Necessary for" means "If \$x\$ is a close friend of Francine, then \$x\$ must be a close friend of Shanika."} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolic: \$\textbackslash{}forall x (F(x) \textbackslash{}rightarrow S(x))\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Conclusion:** "Being an ancestor of Janice is necessary for being a close friend of Francine."} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolic: \$\textbackslash{}forall x (F(x) \textbackslash{}rightarrow A(x))\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**3. Analyze the Logic:**} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   From Premise 2, we know that if someone is a close friend of Francine (\$F\$), they are a cousin of Samantha (\$C\$).} \\
\texttt{*   From Premise 1, we know that if someone is a close friend of Francine (\$F\$), they are either a cousin (\$C\$), an ancestor (\$A\$), or a close friend of Shanika (\$S\$).} \\
\texttt{*   Since we established that \$F\$ implies \$C\$, and \$F\$ implies \$(C \textbackslash{}lor A \textbackslash{}lor S)\$, we can logically deduce that \$F\$ implies \$A\$ or \$S\$ (by the rule of disjunctive syllogism: if \$P\$ is true, and \$P\$ is part of a disjunction \$P \textbackslash{}lor Q \textbackslash{}lor R\$, then \$P\$ implies \$Q\$ or \$R\$).} \\
\texttt{    *   So, \$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow (A \textbackslash{}lor S)\$.} \\
\texttt{*   From Premise 3, we know that \$F\$ implies \$S\$.} \\
\texttt{*   Now we have two implications regarding \$F\$:} \\
\texttt{    1.  \$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow (A \textbackslash{}lor S)\$} \\
\texttt{    2.  \$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow S\$} \\
\texttt{*   If \$F\$ is true, then \$(A \textbackslash{}lor S)\$ is true. Since \$S\$ is also true, the disjunction \$(A \textbackslash{}lor S)\$ is satisfied by \$S\$.} \\
\texttt{*   However, the conclusion claims that \$F\$ implies \$A\$ (\$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow A\$).} \\
\texttt{*   Let's test this with a counter{-}example. Suppose there is a person \$x\$ who is a close friend of Francine (\$F\$ is true).} \\
\texttt{    *   According to Premise 2, \$x\$ is a cousin of Samantha (\$C\$ is true).} \\
\texttt{    *   According to Premise 1, \$x\$ is either \$C\$, \$A\$, or \$S\$. Since \$x\$ is \$C\$, this condition is met regardless of whether \$x\$ is \$A\$ or \$S\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   According to Premise 3, \$x\$ is \$S\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   The premises are satisfied.} \\
\texttt{    *   However, the conclusion states that \$x\$ must be \$A\$. But \$x\$ could simply be \$C\$ (and \$S\$). Being a cousin does not logically force the person to be an ancestor; they could be a cousin *and* a close friend of Shanika.} \\
\texttt{*   Therefore, the truth of \$F\$ does not guarantee the truth of \$A\$. The argument commits the fallacy of **affirming the consequent** (or more accurately, a fallacy of disjunctive syllogism where the disjuncts are not mutually exclusive in a way that forces one to be true over the others, or simply a failure to eliminate the possibility of the disjunct being \$C\$).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Conclusion:**} \\
\texttt{The argument is **invalid**. The premises allow for a scenario where a person is a close friend of Francine, a cousin of Samantha, and a close friend of Shanika, but they are not necessarily an ancestor of Janice.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{A: invalid} \\
