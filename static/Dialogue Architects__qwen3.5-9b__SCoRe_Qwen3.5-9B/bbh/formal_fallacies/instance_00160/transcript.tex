\texttt{    *   Logical form: If someone is *not* a workmate of Leslie, they must be a classmate of Williams.} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolic: \$\textbackslash{}neg W \textbackslash{}to C\$ (where \$W\$ = workmate of Leslie, \$C\$ = classmate of Williams).} \\
\texttt{    *   Equivalent contrapositive: \$\textbackslash{}neg C \textbackslash{}to W\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Premise 2:** "Being a classmate of Williams is necessary for not being a great{-}grandfather of Joseph."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logical form: If someone is *not* a great{-}grandfather of Joseph, they must be a classmate of Williams.} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolic: \$\textbackslash{}neg G \textbackslash{}to C\$ (where \$G\$ = great{-}grandfather of Joseph).} \\
\texttt{    *   Equivalent contrapositive: \$\textbackslash{}neg C \textbackslash{}to G\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Premise 3:** "Every cousin of Tony is a workmate of Leslie and a great{-}grandfather of Joseph."} \\
\texttt{    *   Let \$T\$ represent "is a cousin of Tony".} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolic: \$T \textbackslash{}to (W \textbackslash{}land G)\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   This implies: \$T \textbackslash{}to W\$ AND \$T \textbackslash{}to G\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**2. Analyze the Conclusion:**} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Conclusion:** "Everyone who is a cousin of Tony is a classmate of Williams, too."} \\
\texttt{    *   Symbolic: \$T \textbackslash{}to C\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**3. Evaluate the Deduction:**} \\
\\ 
\texttt{We need to see if the conclusion (\$T \textbackslash{}to C\$) necessarily follows from the premises.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   From Premise 3, we know that for any person who is a cousin of Tony (\$T\$), they are a workmate of Leslie (\$W\$) and a great{-}grandfather of Joseph (\$G\$).} \\
\texttt{*   Now look at Premise 1: \$\textbackslash{}neg W \textbackslash{}to C\$. This tells us what happens if someone is *not* a workmate. It does **not** tell us what happens if someone *is* a workmate. In logic, affirming the consequent (\$W\$) does not allow us to infer the antecedent (\$\textbackslash{}neg W\$) or the original antecedent (\$C\$).} \\
\texttt{    *   Example: "If it rains, the ground is wet." Knowing the ground is wet doesn't prove it rained (it could be a sprinkler). Similarly, knowing someone is a workmate (\$W\$) does not prove they are a classmate (\$C\$).} \\
\texttt{*   Look at Premise 2: \$\textbackslash{}neg G \textbackslash{}to C\$. Same issue. Knowing someone is a great{-}grandfather (\$G\$) does not prove they are a classmate (\$C\$).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Logical Gap:**} \\
\texttt{The premises establish conditions under which one *must* be a classmate of Williams (specifically, if one is *not* a workmate or *not* a great{-}grandfather). The premises provide no information linking the state of "being a workmate/great{-}grandfather" to "being a classmate."} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Therefore, it is logically possible for a cousin of Tony to be a workmate and a great{-}grandfather but *not* a classmate of Williams without contradicting any of the premises. Since the conclusion does not necessarily follow, the argument is invalid.} \\
