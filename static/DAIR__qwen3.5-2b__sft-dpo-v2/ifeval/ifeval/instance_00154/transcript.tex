                \texttt{Generative Adversarial Networks are a type of neural network architecture designed to create realistic data samples. It consists of two main components: a generator and a discriminator. The generator creates fake data while the discriminator tries to identify real data. These two components compete against each other to improve the quality of the generated data. The generator aims to produce data that is indistinguishable from real data, while the discriminator tries to detect any fake data. This adversarial process continues until the generator produces high{-}quality data that is hard to distinguish from real data.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{The architecture of a GAN involves a generator network that takes random noise as input and produces output data, and a discriminator network that takes either real or generated data as input and outputs a probability indicating whether the data is real or fake. The generator and discriminator networks are trained simultaneously through a process called minimax optimization. The generator tries to fool the discriminator, while the discriminator tries to detect any fake data. This competition drives the generator to produce better and better data over time.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{The performance of a GAN depends on the quality of the generated data and the ability of the discriminator to detect fake data. A well{-}tuned GAN can produce data that is highly realistic and convincing. However, if the generator is too good, the discriminator may stop learning and produce low{-}quality outputs. Conversely, if the discriminator is too good, the generator may struggle to produce data that is indistinguishable from real data.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{The quality of the generated data is a critical factor in the success of a GAN. A high{-}quality generated dataset can be used for various applications such as image generation, text generation, and data augmentation. However, if the generated data is lacking in quality or realism, it may not be useful for these applications.} \\
