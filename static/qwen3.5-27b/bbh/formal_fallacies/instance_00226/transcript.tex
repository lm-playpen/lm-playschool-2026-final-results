                \texttt{To determine the validity of the argument, let's break it down into logical notation:} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Premise 1:** "Nobody is neither a classmate of Marisa nor a schoolmate of Karen."} \\
\texttt{*   Let \$C(x)\$ be "x is a classmate of Marisa".} \\
\texttt{*   Let \$S(x)\$ be "x is a schoolmate of Karen".} \\
\texttt{*   "Neither \$C\$ nor \$S\$" translates to \$\textbackslash{}neg C(x) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg S(x)\$.} \\
\texttt{*   "Nobody is..." means there does not exist an \$x\$ such that...} \\
\texttt{*   Formalization: \$\textbackslash{}neg \textbackslash{}exists x (\textbackslash{}neg C(x) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg S(x))\$.} \\
\texttt{*   By De Morgan's laws and quantifier negation, this is equivalent to: \$\textbackslash{}forall x (C(x) \textbackslash{}lor S(x))\$.} \\
\texttt{*   **Meaning:** Everyone is either a classmate of Marisa or a schoolmate of Karen (or both).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Premise 2:** "Not every sister of Ruth is a schoolmate of Karen."} \\
\texttt{*   Let \$R(x)\$ be "x is a sister of Ruth".} \\
\texttt{*   "Not every \$R\$ is \$S\$" means there exists at least one \$x\$ who is \$R\$ but not \$S\$.} \\
\texttt{*   Formalization: \$\textbackslash{}exists x (R(x) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg S(x))\$.} \\
\texttt{*   **Meaning:** There is at least one person who is a sister of Ruth and is NOT a schoolmate of Karen.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Conclusion:** "Somebody is a sister of Ruth and not a classmate of Marisa."} \\
\texttt{*   Formalization: \$\textbackslash{}exists x (R(x) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg C(x))\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Logical Deduction:**} \\
\texttt{1.  From Premise 2, we know there exists a specific individual, let's call them \$a\$, such that \$R(a)\$ is true and \$S(a)\$ is false (\$R(a) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg S(a)\$).} \\
\texttt{2.  From Premise 1, we know that for *everyone* (including \$a\$), \$C(x) \textbackslash{}lor S(x)\$ is true. So, \$C(a) \textbackslash{}lor S(a)\$ must be true.} \\
\texttt{3.  Since we know \$S(a)\$ is false (from step 1), for the disjunction \$C(a) \textbackslash{}lor S(a)\$ to be true, \$C(a)\$ must be true.} \\
\texttt{4.  Therefore, for this individual \$a\$, we have:} \\
\texttt{    *   \$R(a)\$ is true (Sister of Ruth).} \\
\texttt{    *   \$C(a)\$ is true (Classmate of Marisa).} \\
\texttt{5.  The conclusion claims there exists someone who is a sister of Ruth (\$R\$) AND **not** a classmate of Marisa (\$\textbackslash{}neg C\$).} \\
\texttt{6.  Our deduction shows that the person who is a sister of Ruth and not a schoolmate of Karen *must* be a classmate of Marisa.} \\
\texttt{7.  The argument concludes that this person is *not* a classmate of Marisa. This contradicts the necessary result derived from the premises.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Wait, let me re{-}evaluate the conclusion structure.} \\
\texttt{The conclusion is: \$\textbackslash{}exists x (R(x) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg C(x))\$.} \\
\texttt{My deduction showed: For the specific \$x\$ that satisfies Premise 2 (\$R(x) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg S(x)\$), it must be that \$C(x)\$ is true.} \\
\texttt{So, for that specific \$x\$, \$R(x) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg C(x)\$ is **false**.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Does the existence of *another* person satisfy the conclusion?} \\
\texttt{The premises do not guarantee the existence of any other person.} \\
\texttt{Premise 2 guarantees at least one person (\$a\$) who is \$R\$ and \$\textbackslash{}neg S\$.} \\
\texttt{Premise 1 forces that person (\$a\$) to be \$C\$.} \\
\texttt{So we have a person who is \$R\$ and \$C\$.} \\
\texttt{The conclusion requires a person who is \$R\$ and \$\textbackslash{}neg C\$.} \\
\texttt{The premises do not provide any information that forces the existence of a person who is \$R\$ and \$\textbackslash{}neg C\$. In fact, the premises are consistent with a world where *everyone* who is a sister of Ruth is a classmate of Marisa (specifically, the one sister we know of is a classmate).} \\
\texttt{If the premises are true, the conclusion *could* be false (if the only sister of Ruth is the one we identified, who is a classmate of Marisa).} \\
\texttt{Therefore, the conclusion does not necessarily follow.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Let's double check the logic.} \\
\texttt{P1: \$\textbackslash{}forall x (C(x) \textbackslash{}lor S(x))\$} \\
\texttt{P2: \$\textbackslash{}exists x (R(x) \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg S(x))\$} \\

\texttt{\$R(a)\$ is true.} \\
\texttt{\$\textbackslash{}neg S(a)\$ is true.} \\
