\\ 
\texttt{**1. Define the Propositions:**} \\
\texttt{*   \$F\$: Being a close friend of Francine.} \\
\texttt{*   \$C\$: Being a cousin of Samantha.} \\
\texttt{*   \$A\$: Being an ancestor of Janice.} \\
\texttt{*   \$S\$: Being a close friend of Shanika.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**2. Analyze the Premises:**} \\
\texttt{*   **Premise 1:** "Every close friend of Francine is a cousin of Samantha or an ancestor of Janice or a close friend of Shanika."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logic: \$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow (C \textbackslash{}lor A \textbackslash{}lor S)\$} \\
\texttt{*   **Premise 2:** "Every close friend of Francine is a cousin of Samantha."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logic: \$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow C\$} \\
\texttt{*   **Premise 3:** "Being a close friend of Shanika is necessary for being a close friend of Francine."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logic: If \$F\$ is true, then \$S\$ must be true (\$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow S\$).} \\
\texttt{    *   *Note:* The phrasing "X is necessary for Y" means \$Y \textbackslash{}rightarrow X\$. So, \$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow S\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**3. Analyze the Conclusion:**} \\
\texttt{*   **Conclusion:** "Being an ancestor of Janice is necessary for being a close friend of Francine."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logic: \$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow A\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**4. Evaluate the Argument:**} \\
\texttt{*   From **Premise 2**, we know that if someone is a close friend of Francine (\$F\$), they are definitely a cousin of Samantha (\$C\$).} \\
\texttt{*   From **Premise 1**, we know that if someone is a close friend of Francine (\$F\$), they satisfy at least one of the conditions: \$C\$, \$A\$, or \$S\$.} \\
\texttt{*   Since Premise 2 establishes that \$C\$ is always true for any \$F\$, the condition \$(C \textbackslash{}lor A \textbackslash{}lor S)\$ in Premise 1 is automatically satisfied regardless of whether \$A\$ (ancestor of Janice) is true or false.} \\
\texttt{*   Therefore, the truth of \$A\$ is not required for \$F\$ to be true. One can be a close friend of Francine simply because they are a cousin of Samantha, without needing to be an ancestor of Janice.} \\
\texttt{*   Premise 3 (\$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow S\$) also does not force \$A\$ to be true; it only links \$F\$ to \$S\$.} \\
\texttt{*   Since the conclusion (\$F \textbackslash{}rightarrow A\$) does not necessarily follow from the premises (it is possible for \$F\$ to be true while \$A\$ is false), the argument is **invalid**.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{A: invalid} \\
