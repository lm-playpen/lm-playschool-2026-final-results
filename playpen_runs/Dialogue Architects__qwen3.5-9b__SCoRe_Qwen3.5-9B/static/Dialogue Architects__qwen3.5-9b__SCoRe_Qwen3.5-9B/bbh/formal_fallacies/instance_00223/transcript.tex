\texttt{**Analysis:**} \\
\texttt{Let's break down the logical structure of the argument:} \\
\\ 
\texttt{1.  **Premise 1:** No (Expert of Malmö FF \$\textbackslash{}land\$ Ex{-}supporter of Brøndby IF) \$\textbackslash{}rightarrow\$ Ex{-}fan of F91 Dudelange.} \\
\texttt{    *   Logically equivalent to: If someone is an Ex{-}fan of F91 Dudelange, they are NOT (Expert of Malmö FF \$\textbackslash{}land\$ Ex{-}supporter of Brøndby IF).} \\
\texttt{    *   Or: \$\textbackslash{}neg (\textbackslash{}text\{Expert\} \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}Brøndby\}) \textbackslash{}rightarrow \textbackslash{}neg \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}F91\}\$ is false? No, let's stick to standard form: \$\textbackslash{}neg (\textbackslash{}text\{Expert\} \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}Brøndby\}) \textbackslash{}rightarrow \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}F91\}\$ is NOT what it says.} \\
\texttt{    *   Correct translation: \$\textbackslash{}neg (\textbackslash{}text\{Expert\} \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}Brøndby\}) \textbackslash{}rightarrow \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}F91\}\$ is incorrect.} \\
\texttt{    *   Correct translation: \$\textbackslash{}text\{Expert\} \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}Brøndby\} \textbackslash{}rightarrow \textbackslash{}neg \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}F91\}\$. (If you are both, you are not an ex{-}fan of F91).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{2.  **Premise 2:** \$\textbackslash{}neg \textbackslash{}text\{Follower of Stade Rennais\} \textbackslash{}rightarrow \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}fan of F91\}\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   Contrapositive: \$\textbackslash{}neg \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}fan of F91\} \textbackslash{}rightarrow \textbackslash{}text\{Follower of Stade Rennais\}\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{3.  **Conclusion:** \$\textbackslash{}neg (\textbackslash{}text\{Expert\} \textbackslash{}lor \textbackslash{}text\{Ex{-}Brøndby\}) \textbackslash{}rightarrow \textbackslash{}text\{Follower of Stade Rennais\}\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   This translates to: If someone is neither an expert nor an ex{-}supporter, then they are a follower of Stade Rennais.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**The Flaw:**} \\
\texttt{The premises establish a relationship for people who are *both* an expert and an ex{-}supporter (Premise 1). They do not establish anything about people who are *neither*.} \\
\texttt{*   Premise 1 tells us about the group \$(A \textbackslash{}land B)\$.} \\
\texttt{*   Premise 2 tells us about the group \$\textbackslash{}neg C\$.} \\
\texttt{*   The conclusion makes a claim about the group \$\textbackslash{}neg (A \textbackslash{}lor B)\$, which is equivalent to \$(\textbackslash{}neg A \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg B)\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{There is no logical link provided between being "neither an expert nor an ex{-}supporter" and being a "follower of Stade Rennais." One could easily be neither an expert nor an ex{-}supporter, yet still not be a follower of Stade Rennais (and consequently not an ex{-}fan of F91), without contradicting the premises. The argument commits a formal fallacy by assuming that the negation of a disjunction in the antecedent of the conclusion follows from the premises, which only cover specific intersections or the consequent of the second premise.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Therefore, the argument is **invalid**.} \\
