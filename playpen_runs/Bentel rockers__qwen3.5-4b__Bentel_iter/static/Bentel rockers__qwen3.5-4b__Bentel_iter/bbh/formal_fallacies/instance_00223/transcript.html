To determine the validity of this argument, let's break down the premises and the conclusion using logical symbols.

**1. Define the Propositions:**
* $M$: Is an expert of Malmö FF.
* $B$: Is an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF.
* $D$: Is an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange.
* $R$: Is a follower of Stade Rennais FC.

**2. Translate the Premises:**
* **Premise 1:** "No expert of Malmö FF who is an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF is an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange."
* This means: If someone is both $M$ and $B$, they are *not* $D$.
* Logical form: $(M \land B) \to \neg D$
* Contrapositive (equivalent): $D \to \neg(M \land B)$, which is equivalent to $D \to (\neg M \lor \neg B)$.

* **Premise 2:** "Whoever is not a follower of Stade Rennais FC is an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange."
* This means: If someone is not $R$, then they are $D$.
* Logical form: $\neg R \to D$

* **Conclusion:** "Whoever is none of this: an expert of Malmö FF or ex-supporter of Brøndby IF, is a follower of Stade Rennais FC."
* "None of this: $M$ or $B$" translates to $\neg M \land \neg B$.
* The conclusion states: If $(\neg M \land \neg B)$, then $R$.
* Logical form: $(\neg M \land \neg B) \to R$

**3. Analyze the Logic:**
Let's test the conclusion against the premises. We need to see if the premises guarantee the conclusion.

* From Premise 2 ($\neg R \to D$), by contraposition, we get $D \to R$.
* From Premise 1, we have $(M \land B) \to \neg D$. By contraposition, this gives $\neg(\neg D) \to \neg(M \land B)$, which simplifies to $D \to \neg(M \land B)$.
* Note: $\neg(M \land B)$ is logically equivalent to $(\neg M \lor \neg B)$.
* So, combining these: If $D$, then $R$ AND if $D$, then $(\neg M \lor \neg B)$.
* Therefore, $D \to (R \land (\neg M \lor \neg B))$.

Now, look at the conclusion we need to prove: $(\neg M \land \neg B) \to R$.
* The condition in the conclusion is $(\neg M \land \neg B)$.
* From our derived logic, we know that if $D$ is true, then $(\neg M \lor \neg B)$ is true.
* However, the conclusion requires the stronger condition $(\neg M \land \neg B)$ (both must be false) to trigger $R$.
* The premises only tell us that if someone is $D$, they fail *at least one* of the conditions $M$ or $B$. They do not tell us that if someone fails *both* conditions, they must be $R$.
* It is possible for someone to be neither an expert of Malmö FF nor an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF ($\neg M \land \neg B$) without being an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange ($D$). In that case, the chain $\neg R \to D$ doesn't help us prove $R$ because the antecedent ($\neg R$) isn't necessarily true based on the other premises.

* Antecedent True: $\neg M \land \neg B$ (So $M$ is False, $B$ is False).

If $D$ is True, Premise 1 ($ (M \land B) \to \neg D $) requires $(M \land B)$ to be False.

1. $(F \land F) \to \neg T$ => $F \to F$ (True).
