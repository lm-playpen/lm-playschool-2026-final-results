\\ 
\texttt{**1. Define the Propositions:**} \\
\texttt{*   \$M\$: is an expert of Malmö FF} \\
\texttt{*   \$B\$: is an ex{-}supporter of Brøndby IF} \\
\texttt{*   \$D\$: is an ex{-}fan of F91 Dudelange} \\
\texttt{*   \$R\$: is a follower of Stade Rennais FC} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**2. Translate the Premises:**} \\
\texttt{*   **Premise 1:** "No expert of Malmö FF who is an ex{-}supporter of Brøndby IF is an ex{-}fan of F91 Dudelange."} \\
\texttt{    *   This means: If someone is both \$M\$ and \$B\$, they are not \$D\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   Logic: \$(M \textbackslash{}land B) \textbackslash{}to \textbackslash{}neg D\$} \\
\texttt{    *   Contrapositive: \$D \textbackslash{}to \textbackslash{}neg(M \textbackslash{}land B)\$, which is equivalent to \$D \textbackslash{}to (\textbackslash{}neg M \textbackslash{}lor \textbackslash{}neg B)\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Premise 2:** "Whoever is not a follower of Stade Rennais FC is an ex{-}fan of F91 Dudelange."} \\
\texttt{    *   Logic: \$\textbackslash{}neg R \textbackslash{}to D\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   **Conclusion:** "Whoever is none of this: an expert of Malmö FF or ex{-}supporter of Brøndby IF, is a follower of Stade Rennais FC."} \\
\texttt{    *   "None of this: \$M\$ or \$B\$" means \$\textbackslash{}neg(M \textbackslash{}lor B)\$, which is equivalent to \$\textbackslash{}neg M \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg B\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   The conclusion states: If \$\textbackslash{}neg M \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg B\$, then \$R\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   Logic: \$(\textbackslash{}neg M \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg B) \textbackslash{}to R\$} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**3. Analyze the Logical Flow:**} \\
\texttt{Let's test if the conclusion follows from the premises. We can use a proof by contradiction or a counter{-}example.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{*   Assume the antecedent of the conclusion is true: Someone is **not** an expert of Malmö FF (\$\textbackslash{}neg M\$) AND **not** an ex{-}supporter of Brøndby IF (\$\textbackslash{}neg B\$).} \\
\texttt{    *   So, we have a person who is \$\textbackslash{}neg M\$ and \$\textbackslash{}neg B\$.} \\
\texttt{*   Does this person satisfy the condition for \$D\$ (ex{-}fan of F91 Dudelange)?} \\
\texttt{    *   Premise 1 says: If \$(M \textbackslash{}land B)\$, then \$\textbackslash{}neg D\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   Our person has \$\textbackslash{}neg M\$ and \$\textbackslash{}neg B\$, so the condition \$(M \textbackslash{}land B)\$ is **false**.} \\
\texttt{    *   In logic, if the "if" part of a conditional statement is false, the statement tells us nothing about the "then" part. The person could be \$D\$, or they could be \$\textbackslash{}neg D\$. Premise 1 does not force them to be \$D\$ or \$\textbackslash{}neg D\$.} \\
\texttt{*   Does this person satisfy the condition for \$R\$ (follower of Stade Rennais FC)?} \\
\texttt{    *   Premise 2 says: If \$\textbackslash{}neg R\$, then \$D\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   This is equivalent to: If \$\textbackslash{}neg D\$, then \$R\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   For the conclusion \$(\textbackslash{}neg M \textbackslash{}land \textbackslash{}neg B) \textbackslash{}to R\$ to be valid, we would need to prove that anyone with \$\textbackslash{}neg M\$ and \$\textbackslash{}neg B\$ must be \$R\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   To prove \$R\$, we would need to prove \$\textbackslash{}neg D\$ (via the contrapositive of Premise 2).} \\
\texttt{    *   However, as established in the previous step, Premise 1 does not guarantee that a person with \$\textbackslash{}neg M\$ and \$\textbackslash{}neg B\$ is \$\textbackslash{}neg D\$. They could simply be an ex{-}fan of F91 Dudelange (\$D\$) without being an expert of Malmö FF or an ex{-}supporter of Brøndby IF.} \\
\texttt{    *   If this person is \$D\$, then Premise 2 (\$\textbackslash{}neg R \textbackslash{}to D\$) does not trigger a conclusion about \$R\$. The person could be \$\textbackslash{}neg R\$ (not a follower) and still be \$D\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Counter{-}Example Construction:**} \\
\texttt{Imagine a person, "Bob":} \\
\texttt{*   Bob is **not** an expert of Malmö FF (\$\textbackslash{}neg M\$).} \\
\texttt{*   Bob is **not** an ex{-}supporter of Brøndby IF (\$\textbackslash{}neg B\$).} \\
\texttt{*   Bob **is** an ex{-}fan of F91 Dudelange (\$D\$).} \\
\texttt{*   Bob is **not** a follower of Stade Rennais FC (\$\textbackslash{}neg R\$).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Check the premises with Bob:} \\
\texttt{1.  Is "No \$(M \textbackslash{}land B)\$ is \$D\$" true? Yes, because Bob is not \$(M \textbackslash{}land B)\$, so the rule doesn't apply to him. The rule is vacuously true for Bob.} \\
\texttt{2.  Is "If not \$R\$ then \$D\$" true? Yes, because Bob is \$\textbackslash{}neg R\$ and he is \$D\$.} \\

\\ 
\texttt{Since we can construct a scenario where the premises are true but the conclusion is false, the argument is **invalid**.} \\
