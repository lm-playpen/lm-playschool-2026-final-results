Q: "Is Fred a fan of Liverpool? Are supporters of Real Madrid devotees of PSG? In European football, it is sometimes difficult to keep track of the mutual admiration and dislike. The following argument seeks to clarify some such relations: First of all, no expert of Malmö FF who is an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF is an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange. Next, whoever is not a follower of Stade Rennais FC is an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange. So, necessarily, whoever is none of this: an expert of Malmö FF or ex-supporter of Brøndby IF, is a follower of Stade Rennais FC."
Is the argument, given the explicitly stated premises, deductively valid or invalid?

- valid
- invalid
A:

To determine the validity of this argument, let's break down the premises and the conclusion using logical notation.

**1. Define the Propositions:**
* $M$: is an expert of Malmö FF
* $B$: is an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF
* $D$: is an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange
* $R$: is a follower of Stade Rennais FC

**2. Translate the Premises:**
* **Premise 1:** "No expert of Malmö FF who is an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF is an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange."
* This means: If someone is both $M$ and $B$, they are not $D$.
* Logic: $(M \land B) \to \neg D$
* Contrapositive: $D \to \neg(M \land B)$, which is equivalent to $D \to (\neg M \lor \neg B)$.

* **Premise 2:** "Whoever is not a follower of Stade Rennais FC is an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange."
* Logic: $\neg R \to D$

* **Conclusion:** "Whoever is none of this: an expert of Malmö FF or ex-supporter of Brøndby IF, is a follower of Stade Rennais FC."
* "None of this: $M$ or $B$" means $\neg(M \lor B)$, which is equivalent to $\neg M \land \neg B$.
* The conclusion states: If $\neg M \land \neg B$, then $R$.
* Logic: $(\neg M \land \neg B) \to R$

**3. Analyze the Logical Flow:**
Let's test if the conclusion follows from the premises. We can use a proof by contradiction or a counter-example.

* Assume the antecedent of the conclusion is true: Someone is **not** an expert of Malmö FF ($\neg M$) AND **not** an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF ($\neg B$).
* So, we have a person who is $\neg M$ and $\neg B$.
* Does this person satisfy the condition for $D$ (ex-fan of F91 Dudelange)?
* Premise 1 says: If $(M \land B)$, then $\neg D$.
* Our person has $\neg M$ and $\neg B$, so the condition $(M \land B)$ is **false**.
* In logic, if the "if" part of a conditional statement is false, the statement tells us nothing about the "then" part. The person could be $D$, or they could be $\neg D$. Premise 1 does not force them to be $D$ or $\neg D$.
* Does this person satisfy the condition for $R$ (follower of Stade Rennais FC)?
* Premise 2 says: If $\neg R$, then $D$.
* This is equivalent to: If $\neg D$, then $R$.
* For the conclusion $(\neg M \land \neg B) \to R$ to be valid, we would need to prove that anyone with $\neg M$ and $\neg B$ must be $R$.
* To prove $R$, we would need to prove $\neg D$ (via the contrapositive of Premise 2).
* However, as established in the previous step, Premise 1 does not guarantee that a person with $\neg M$ and $\neg B$ is $\neg D$. They could simply be an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange ($D$) without being an expert of Malmö FF or an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF.
* If this person is $D$, then Premise 2 ($\neg R \to D$) does not trigger a conclusion about $R$. The person could be $\neg R$ (not a follower) and still be $D$.

**Counter-Example Construction:**
Imagine a person, "Bob":
* Bob is **not** an expert of Malmö FF ($\neg M$).
* Bob is **not** an ex-supporter of Brøndby IF ($\neg B$).
* Bob **is** an ex-fan of F91 Dudelange ($D$).
* Bob is **not** a follower of Stade Rennais FC ($\neg R$).

Check the premises with Bob:
1. Is "No $(M \land B)$ is $D$" true? Yes, because Bob is not $(M \land B)$, so the rule doesn't apply to him. The rule is vacuously true for Bob.
2. Is "If not $R$ then $D$" true? Yes, because Bob is $\neg R$ and he is $D$.
3. Is the conclusion "If not $M$ and not $B$ then $R$" true? No. Bob is $\neg M$ and $\neg B$, but he is $\neg R$.

Since we can construct a scenario where the premises are true but the conclusion is false, the argument is **invalid**.
