\\ 
\texttt{**Premise 1:** Merle is a cousin of Annie.} \\
\texttt{*   \$M = \textbackslash{}text\{Cousin of Annie\}\$ (True)} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Premise 2:** Whoever is not both a classmate of Hazel and a cousin of Annie is a great{-}grandmother of Doria.} \\
\texttt{*   Let \$C\$ = Classmate of Hazel.} \\
\texttt{*   Let \$M\$ = Cousin of Annie.} \\
\texttt{*   Let \$G\$ = Great{-}grandmother of Doria.} \\
\texttt{*   The statement translates to: \$\textbackslash{}neg(C \textbackslash{}land M) \textbackslash{}rightarrow G\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   This is logically equivalent to: \$C \textbackslash{}land M \textbackslash{}rightarrow \textbackslash{}neg G\$ (If someone is both a classmate and a cousin, they are *not* a great{-}grandmother).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Conclusion:** It is false that Merle is a great{-}grandmother of Doria.} \\
\texttt{*   \$\textbackslash{}neg G(M)\$ (Merle is not a great{-}grandmother of Doria).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Analysis:**} \\
\texttt{1.  We know from Premise 1 that Merle is a cousin of Annie (\$M\$ is true).} \\
\texttt{2.  To conclude that Merle is *not* a great{-}grandmother (\$\textbackslash{}neg G\$), we would need to establish that the condition in the contrapositive of Premise 2 is met. That is, we would need to prove that Merle is a classmate of Hazel (\$C\$ is true).} \\
\texttt{3.  If Merle were *not* a classmate of Hazel, then the condition \$\textbackslash{}neg(C \textbackslash{}land M)\$ would be true (since \$C\$ is false), which would trigger the rule in Premise 2, making Merle a great{-}grandmother (\$G\$).} \\
\texttt{4.  However, the premises do not state whether Merle is a classmate of Hazel or not.} \\
\texttt{    *   **Scenario A:** Merle is a classmate of Hazel. Then \$C \textbackslash{}land M\$ is true. By the contrapositive of Premise 2, Merle is not a great{-}grandmother. The conclusion holds.} \\
\texttt{    *   **Scenario B:** Merle is *not* a classmate of Hazel. Then \$\textbackslash{}neg(C \textbackslash{}land M)\$ is true. By Premise 2, Merle *is* a great{-}grandmother. The conclusion ("It is false that Merle is a great{-}grandmother") would be false.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Since there is a possible scenario (Scenario B) where the premises are true but the conclusion is false, the argument does not guarantee the truth of the conclusion based solely on the premises.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{Therefore, the argument is **invalid**.} \\
