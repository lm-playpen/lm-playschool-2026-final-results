\texttt{*   Scenario B: \$E \textbackslash{}to Y\$ is negative. The negative observed correlation is due to the direct causal effect (and potentially reinforced by \$U\$).} \\

\texttt{In causal inference, if the data is consistent with both a positive and a negative causal effect, and no further information (like the strength of the confounder or the direct effect) is provided, we cannot conclude that the degree *will* increase the chance. The question asks "Will...", implying a definitive prediction based on the provided facts. The observed association is negative (High \$E\$ \$\textbackslash{}leftrightarrow\$ Low \$Y\$). If the causal effect were positive, the confounder would have to be very strong. If the causal effect were negative, the confounder could be weak or non{-}existent (though the problem says it exists). Without knowing the relative strengths, the causal direction is ambiguous.} \\
