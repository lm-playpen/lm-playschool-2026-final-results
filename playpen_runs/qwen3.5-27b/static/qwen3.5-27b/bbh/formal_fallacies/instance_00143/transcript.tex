\texttt{    *   Statement: \$C(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$ is **True**.} \\

\texttt{2.  **Premise 2**: Whoever is not both a classmate of Hazel and a cousin of Annie is a great{-}grandmother of Doria.} \\
\texttt{    *   Let \$H(x)\$ be "x is a classmate of Hazel".} \\
\texttt{    *   Let \$G(x)\$ be "x is a great{-}grandmother of Doria".} \\
\texttt{    *   The condition "not both a classmate of Hazel and a cousin of Annie" translates to \$\textbackslash{}neg (H(x) \textbackslash{}land C(x))\$.} \\
\texttt{    *   The logical form is: \$\textbackslash{}forall x, \textbackslash{}neg (H(x) \textbackslash{}land C(x)) \textbackslash{}rightarrow G(x)\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{3.  **Conclusion**: It is false that Merle is a great{-}grandmother of Doria.} \\
\texttt{    *   Statement: \$\textbackslash{}neg G(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$.} \\
\\ 
\texttt{**Analysis**:} \\
\texttt{*   From Premise 1, we know \$C(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$ is true.} \\
\texttt{*   To trigger the implication in Premise 2 for Merle (i.e., to conclude \$G(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$), the antecedent \$\textbackslash{}neg (H(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}) \textbackslash{}land C(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}))\$ must be true.} \\
\texttt{*   However, we do not know the truth value of \$H(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$ (whether Merle is a classmate of Hazel).} \\
\texttt{    *   **Scenario A**: If Merle is *not* a classmate of Hazel (\$H(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$ is False), then \$(H(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}) \textbackslash{}land C(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}))\$ is False. Consequently, \$\textbackslash{}neg (H(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}) \textbackslash{}land C(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}))\$ is True. By Modus Ponens, \$G(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$ would be True.} \\
\texttt{    *   **Scenario B**: If Merle *is* a classmate of Hazel (\$H(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$ is True), then \$(H(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}) \textbackslash{}land C(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}))\$ is True. Consequently, \$\textbackslash{}neg (H(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}) \textbackslash{}land C(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\}))\$ is False. The implication does not tell us anything about \$G(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$ in this case (the antecedent is false, so the consequent can be either true or false).} \\
\\ 
\texttt{The argument concludes definitively that \$\textbackslash{}neg G(\textbackslash{}text\{Merle\})\$ (Merle is *not* a great{-}grandmother).} \\

Since there is a possible scenario where the premises are true but the conclusion is false (Scenario A), the argument is not deductively valid. The conclusion does not necessarily follow from the premises.} \\
